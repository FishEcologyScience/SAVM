SAVM Help

Getting Started

This tool is intended to support researchers, environmental managers, and conservation practitioners who wish to assess potential SAV distribution and cover in their area of interest. The SAVM application applies statistical models to predict the probability of SAV occurrence and potential cover. The application offers three statistical approaches (Random Forest, Generalized Additive Models, and Linear Mixed Models). These models are based on data collected through extensive field surveys of SAV distribution patterns in portions of the Great Lakes (Croft-White et al. 2022). The models use water depth (m) and fetch (with 15 km set as the maximum fetch distance) as primary predictors, recognizing that these environmental factors strongly influence SAV colonization and growth. Post-hoc adjustments for water clarity (Secchi depth), substrate limitations, and user-defined constraints can also be imposed. Development of the original random forest model and potential use cases for this tool are outlined in Croft-White et al. (2022). Models have since been updated with additional field data.

Importantly, model development and validation occurred solely in Lake Ontario and Severn Sound, Lake Huron. At this time, application of this tool outside of these areas is not recommended without independent verification of model predictions.

Key Features of the Application
  • Multiple Statistical Methods: Choose between Random Forest, Generalized Additive Models (GAM), or Linear Mixed Models (LMM) for both presence/absence and percent cover predictions. Each model type has benefits and limitations and additional details on these factors as well as details on the accuracy of the updated models will be provided in a future technical report.
  • Flexible Column Mapping: Easily map your data columns to model requirements, with automatic detection of common naming conventions and also example templates.
  • Post-hoc Refinements: Incorporate water clarity (Secchi depth), substrate limitations, and custom constraints to refine predictions. Results from such adjustments are coded with “post_hoc”.
  • Interactive Visualization: Explore predictions on interactive maps with tooltips showing detailed information for each point.
  • Integrated Fetch Calculator: Calculate fetch directly within the application using lake polygons and customizable parameters.
Step Details
1 Data Input:  Upload your spatial data (GeoPackage, GeoJSON, or Shapefile) containing location information and environmental variables. Alternately, toggle to “Manual Entry” to select point(s) or areas of interest (polygon).
2 Fetch Calculation: Calculate fetch distances for your study sites using the integrated fetch calculator with customizable parameters (maximum fetch distance (km), number of bearings, and wind weighting). Base water layers for the Great Lakes are available within the app, but users can also supply their own water polygon. Note: processing time will increase with increasing file size, number of points, and number of fetch bearings.
3 Depth Extraction: Extract depth information from user-supplied raster sources (GeoTIFF (.tif), NetCDF (.nc), ESRI Grid (.grd), ASCII (.asc).) or assign fixed depth values for the entire study area.
4 Model Application: Apply SAV prediction models (presence/absence and percent cover) by selecting your preferred statistical method, specifying predictor columns, and configuring post-hoc adjustments.
    Post-hoc Adjustments:
  • Substrate: If the user has information about potential limitations related to substrate composition at their sites (e.g., >75% sand; Barko and Smart 1986 or >20% organic matter; Capers and Les 2005), a “substrate” column can be added to the input data where 1 = no limitations and 0 = No SAV.
  • Secchi depth: When Secchi depth data are available, SAVM calculates the maximum depth of plant colonization (Vmax) using equations presented in Chambers and Kalff (1985). This is used as a post-hoc adjustment of model predictions to account for the effect of light limitations on SAV distribution. The user can provide Secchi depth information in the input table or select an overall Secchi depth value to apply to all data.
  • User-supplied Limitation: If the user has information on “other” factors that may limit growth at a site (e.g., substrate toxicity), a “limitation” column can be added to the input data where 1 = no limitations and 0 = No SAV.
5 Results & Export: Review model predictions, visualize results on interactive maps, and export your data for further analysis.
