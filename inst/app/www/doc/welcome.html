The Submerged Aquatic Vegetation Model (SAVM) application is a decision-support tool designed to predict the presence or absence and percent cover of submerged aquatic vegetation (SAV) in freshwater environments based on two simple parameters – depth and fetch. This tool is intended to support researchers, environmental managers, and conservation practitioners who wish to assess potential SAV distribution and cover in their area of interest. Development of the original random forest model and potential use cases for this tool are outlined in Croft-White et al. (2022 – see link in ‘Help’ section); however, models have since been updated with additional field data. Importantly, model development and validation occurred solely in Lake Ontario and Severn Sound, Lake Huron. At this time, application of this tool outside of these areas is not recommended without independent verification of model predictions.

What does SAVM do?
SAVM applies statistical models to predict the probability of SAV occurrence and potential cover based on water depth and fetch. The application offers three statistical approaches (Random Forest, GAM, and Linear Mixed Models) and includes optional post-hoc adjustments for water clarity (Secchi depth), substrate limitations, and user-defined constraints.
